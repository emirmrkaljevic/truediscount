The EU 30-day lowest price rule, country by country

One rule for every EU country, many languages on the price tag. Here is what the rule says and the wording shops use for it in each market.

What the rule says

Article 6a of Directive 98/6/EC, added by the Omnibus Directive 2019/2161 and applied since 28 May 2022, says that any announced price reduction must refer to the lowest price the shop charged in the 30 days before the reduction. The EU Court of Justice confirmed in case C-330/23 (Aldi Süd, 2024) that a percentage discount must be calculated from that price too.

Every EU country has written the rule into national law (in Germany, for example, it is section 11 of the Price Indication Ordinance, PAngV). Countries may allow some exceptions, such as for perishable goods, new products on sale for less than 30 days, or progressive reductions during one sale, so details differ. Check your national consumer authority for the exact rules.

What to do with it

  1. Compare the sale price with the 30-day lowest price, not with the crossed-out price.
  2. If the crossed-out price is an RRP (UVP, adviesprijs, PVPR and so on), it says nothing about whether the price really dropped.
  3. If the "old price" was never the shop's 30-day lowest, report it to your national consumer authority. The European Commission's consumer rights page lists where to go.

Checking it automatically

truediscount is a free, open-source extension for Chrome and Firefox that reads these 30-day labels in about 15 languages, flags RRP comparisons and keeps a local price history that never leaves your browser.
