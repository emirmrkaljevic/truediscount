# Fake discount tricks

Seven tricks shops use to make a discount look bigger, and how to spot each one. Back to the [guides index](README.md) or the [README](../../README.md). Web version: [fake discount tricks](https://emirmrkaljevic.github.io/truediscount/guides/fake-discount-tricks.html).

1. **Raising the price just before the sale.** The price goes up for a few days, then is "cut" back. Compare the crossed-out price with the 30-day lowest price line.
2. **Calculating the percentage from the wrong price.** The 30-day low is printed in small text, but "-40%" is calculated from the price the day before. The EU Court of Justice ruled this is not allowed (C-330/23). Work out the discount yourself from the 30-day low.
3. **Using the manufacturer's recommended price (RRP, UVP, PVC).** An RRP is not a price the shop charged. The Dutch ACM says a shop must not cross it out or add a discount percentage unless it was the shop's own 30-day lowest price.
4. **Inflating the "was" price overnight.** The sale price stays the same while the crossed-out price goes up. ACM found a "from" price that jumped from 149.99 to 199.99 euro while the sale price stayed at 99.99 euro.
5. **A changing or wrong 30-day price.** Poland's UOKiK found a marketplace where the 30-day price changed day by day during one promotion while the selling price stayed the same.
6. **Words instead of numbers.** "Deal", "price highlight" or "Black Friday price" with no prior price. The Court said such claims also count as reduction announcements, so a prior price should be shown.
7. **Comparing with other shops.** "Was 199 euro elsewhere" is a price comparison, not a reduction by this shop. Ask whether this shop ever charged that price.

## What the 30-day rule does not cover

- Price comparisons with other shops, or an RRP not presented as the shop's own reduction (general unfair practices law still applies).
- Services, and general low prices that never claim to be a reduction.
- Countries may set a different reference for perishable goods, products on the market for less than 30 days, and progressive reductions during one continuous sale.
