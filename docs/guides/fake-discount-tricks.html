Fake discount tricks and how to spot them

Seven tricks shops use to make discounts look bigger, and what the EU 30-day lowest price rule says about each.

Under EU law (Article 6a of Directive 98/6/EC), a shop that announces a price reduction must show the lowest price it charged in the 30 days before the reduction, and the EU Court of Justice confirmed the discount must be calculated from that price. Here are the tricks we see most and how to spot them.

1. Raising the price just before the sale

The shop raises the price for a few days, then "cuts" it back. How to spot it: compare the crossed-out price with the "lowest price in the last 30 days" line. If the sale price equals or beats that line by very little, the discount is mostly fake. A price history tool shows it at once.

2. Calculating the percentage from the wrong price

The shop prints the 30-day lowest price somewhere small but calculates "-40%" from the price the day before. The Court ruled this is not allowed. How to spot it: work out the discount yourself from the 30-day low.

3. Using the manufacturer's recommended price (RRP, UVP, PVC)

A "recommended retail price" is not a price the shop charged, so it is not a prior price. The Dutch ACM says a shop that shows such a price must explain what it means and must not cross it out or add a discount percentage unless it was the shop's own 30-day lowest price. How to spot it: look for words like RRP, UVP, recommended price or Adviesprijs next to the crossed-out number.

4. Inflating the "was" price overnight

The sale price stays the same while the crossed-out price goes up, so the discount looks bigger. ACM found a case where the "from" price jumped from 149.99 to 199.99 euro while the sale price stayed at 99.99 euro. How to spot it: check the same product a day or two later, or use a tool that remembers prices.

5. A changing or wrong 30-day price

UOKiK found a marketplace where the "lowest price in 30 days" changed day by day during the same promotion. How to spot it: if the 30-day figure moves while the price does not, be suspicious.

6. Words instead of numbers

"Deal", "price highlight" or "Black Friday price" with no prior price at all. The Court said promotional claims that stress how good the price is also count as reduction announcements. How to spot it: a real reduction should show a prior price.

7. Comparing with other shops

"Was 199 euro elsewhere" or "average market price" is a price comparison, not a reduction by this shop. How to spot it: ask whether this shop ever charged that price.

FAQ

Is a crossed-out RRP a real discount?

Not under the 30-day rule. A recommended retail price is not a price the shop charged. The discount should be calculated from the shop's own lowest price in the previous 30 days.

How do I know if a Black Friday discount is fake?

Compare the sale price with the lowest price in the last 30 days that the shop must show, and calculate the discount from that number. If the shop raised the price shortly before the sale, the real saving is small or zero.

Does the 30-day rule apply to services?

No. Article 6a of the Price Indication Directive covers price reductions on goods. Misleading prices for services can still be challenged under unfair commercial practices law.
