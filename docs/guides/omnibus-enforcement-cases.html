Fake discount fines: real EU enforcement cases

Courts and consumer authorities are enforcing the 30-day lowest price rule. Here are verified cases, each linked to its official source.

Since 28 May 2022, Article 6a of Directive 98/6/EC (added by the Omnibus Directive 2019/2161) requires every announced price reduction to show the prior price, meaning the lowest price the shop charged in at least the 30 days before the reduction. Below are cases where courts or national authorities acted on fake or badly shown "old prices". Every case links to the official source. We only list cases we could check on the authority's or court's own site.

EU Court of Justice: Aldi Süd (C-330/23, 26 September 2024)

A German consumer association challenged Aldi Süd brochures that showed a discount calculated from the price just before the offer, while separately printing a lower 30-day lowest price. The Court ruled that a percentage reduction, or a promotional claim such as "price highlight", must be calculated from the lowest price in the previous 30 days. Simply printing that price next to a discount based on another price is not enough.

Sources: CJEU press release No 152/24, full judgment on EUR-Lex.

Netherlands: ACM fines five web shops (decided 2 May 2024)

The Netherlands Authority for Consumers and Markets (ACM) fined five online shops a total of 621,000 euro for fake discounts based on a misleading "from" price: Koopjedeal (163,000 euro), Leen Bakker (130,000 euro), JYSK (112,500 euro), G-Star (110,000 euro) and Tommy Hilfiger (105,500 euro). ACM gave examples such as a product shown "from 699 for 629 euro" that had cost 539 euro during an earlier promotion in the previous 30 days, and a "from" price raised from 149.99 to 199.99 euro overnight while the sale price stayed at 99.99 euro.

On 11 November 2025 the court upheld the fines against Leen Bakker and Koopjedeal, and ACM reports those rulings are now final.

Sources: ACM: fines for fake discounts (Dutch), ACM: court confirms fines (Dutch).

Poland: UOKiK fines Zalando and Temu (announced 12 January 2026)

Poland's competition and consumer authority UOKiK fined Zalando SE 30,945,000 zloty and Whaleco Technology Limited, the operator of Temu, 5,910,000 zloty over the 30-day lowest price. For Zalando, UOKiK found missing 30-day prices and discount sizes shown incorrectly in different parts of the site. For Temu, it found 30-day prices that were wrong, or that changed day by day during the same promotion while the selling price stayed the same. UOKiK says both decisions are not final and can be appealed in court.

Source: UOKiK press release (English).

Italy: AGCM fines Tannico (17 November 2025)

Italy's competition and consumer authority AGCM fined online wine shop Tannico 150,000 euro because many products advertised as "on offer" had "promotional" prices equal to or higher than the lowest price charged in the previous 30 days. The company changed its site and app in July 2025, during the investigation.

Source: AGCM press release PS12820 (Italian).

Italy: AGCM fines Materassi Marion (23 July 2025)

AGCM fined Emme Group, owner of the Materassi Marion brand, 3 million euro for an unfair practice that included TV promotions showing a large discount from a crossed-out "full price" that was never charged, without showing the lowest price of the previous 30 days. The case also covered aggressive selling during home visits, so the fine is not only about the price display.

Source: AGCM press release PS12706 (Italian).

What these cases have in common

  • The crossed-out price must be a price the shop really charged, and the lowest one in the last 30 days.
  • The percentage must be calculated from that price, not from a list price or the price the day before.
  • Authorities look at the whole site, including category pages and ads, not just the product page.

FAQ

Has the EU Court of Justice ruled on the 30-day lowest price?

Yes. In case C-330/23 Aldi Süd, decided on 26 September 2024, the Court held that an announced percentage reduction or promotional price claim must be calculated from the lowest price the trader charged in the previous 30 days.

Have shops actually been fined for fake old prices?

Yes. Examples include the Dutch ACM fining five web shops 621,000 euro in total in 2024, Poland's UOKiK fining Zalando and Temu nearly 37 million zloty in decisions announced in January 2026, and Italy's AGCM fining Tannico 150,000 euro in November 2025.

Are these decisions final?

Not all of them. The Dutch court upheld the ACM fines against Leen Bakker and Koopjedeal and ACM says those rulings are final. UOKiK says its Zalando and Temu decisions are not final and can be appealed.
